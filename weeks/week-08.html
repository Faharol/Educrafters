A

Section A, part by part

Five parts, one passage. Each part tests a different reading skill, so each needs a different strategy.

Part 1 · 4 marks Gapped text

Four sentences removed. Six offered. Two are distractors.

  1. Read around the gap

    Read the sentence before and the sentence after. The missing sentence must connect to both.

  2. Look for the links

    Pronouns (this, these, it), linkers (however, consequently) and repeated nouns tell you what must come before and after.

  3. Rule out the distractors

    A distractor is usually on-topic but too strong, too general, or points the wrong way (“replaced” when the passage says “supports”).

  4. Use each letter once

    If a letter fits two gaps, one of your other choices is wrong. Check the pair.

An option is clearly on the same topic, but it says the technology “replaced” engineers while the passage says it “supports” them. What is it most likely to be?

Part 2 · 5 marks Matching headings

Five paragraphs, seven headings, two extra

01

Find the main idea, not a detail

A heading summarises the whole paragraph. A detail-heading that matches one sentence is usually a trap.

02

Read first and last sentences

The topic sentence and concluding sentence carry the paragraph’s job: problem, benefit, method, warning, future.

03

Match the function word

“Barriers”, “applications”, “ensuring”, “comparing”: the heading’s first noun tells you what kind of paragraph it names.

04

Do the easy ones first

Then the extra headings become easier to see.

Tap every card to earn the XP.

A heading matches one sentence in the middle of a paragraph perfectly. What should you suspect?

Part 3 · 4 marks Multiple choice

Meaning, detail, purpose and attitude

Four questions: the meaning of a word, a detail, the writer’s purpose, the writer’s attitude.

Locate, then read

The question tells you the paragraph. Go there. Do not answer from memory.

Purpose questions

“Why does the writer…?” Look at what the paragraph does: warns, contrasts, gives an example, qualifies a claim.

Attitude questions

“Cautiously positive”, “strongly critical”: match the hedging in the passage.

“Nevertheless… expected to play an increasingly important role”

This is cautious optimism.

Eliminate extremes

Options with “completely”, “all”, “never” are usually wrong for a hedged academic text.

A passage ends: “Nevertheless, the technology is expected to play an increasingly important role.” What is the writer’s attitude?

Part 4 · 3 marks Vocabulary in context

Three definitions. Find the word in the paragraph named.

01

Match the word class

A definition beginning “to…” wants a verb. “The knowledge and ability…” wants a noun.

02

Copy exactly

Spelling and form as in the passage. “Monitored” is not “monitor” if the passage says “monitored”.

03

Use the paragraph number

The answer is in that paragraph and nowhere else. Scan it; do not reread the whole text.

Look for a verb
Look for a noun
Part 5 · 4 marks Short answer

Two questions, two reasons each

Answer in your own words, in complete sentences.

Two reasons means two marks

Number them or separate them clearly. One long reason scores one.

Paraphrase, do not copy

Copying the passage word for word does not show understanding. Use the Week 3 moves.

Answer the question asked

“Why is it challenging for SMEs?” wants reasons about SMEs, not a summary of the paragraph.

Full sentences

Subject, verb, and the reason.

Preparing for the Workplace (Week 2)

  • Why a résumé must be tailored to the job.
  • Rewrite a claim (“excellent leadership skills”) as evidence: what you did, at what scale, with what result.

Revise: skills-based hiring, the SKILL, EVIDENCE, IMPACT pattern, strong action verbs.

Revision checklist

  • I can name the four barriers: organisational, information overload, fear of superiors, inappropriate media.
  • I can list the features of technical communication.
  • I can explain each letter of the CMAPP model.
  • I can explain why a résumé must be tailored to the job.
  • I can turn a claim into SKILL, EVIDENCE, IMPACT with a strong action verb.
Practice Section B · Part 2 skill

Turn a claim into evidence

The pattern

SKILL → EVIDENCE (what you did, at what scale) → IMPACT (with what result). Start with a strong action verb.

Claim: “I have excellent leadership skills.”

Why is “excellent leadership skills” weak on a résumé?

Rewrite “excellent leadership skills” about yourself. Say what you did, at what scale, and with what result. Use a strong action verb.

Check your answer

Check your sentence has all three pieces:

  • Action verb at the start (for example led, coordinated, organised).
  • Scale: how many people, how much money, how long.
  • Result: what changed because of you.

If one piece is missing, you still have a claim, not evidence.

On the day

A timing plan for the 90 minutes, and the rules of the test room.

Timing plan

A timing plan for 90 minutes

Section A is worth twice Section B. Give it twice the time, and no more.

  1. 0 to 6 min

    Read the whole passage once. Number the paragraphs. Mark the gaps.

  2. 6 to 50 min

    Section A, Parts 1 to 5, in order. Easy items first within each part.

  3. 50 to 76 min

    Section B, Part 1 then Part 2. Full sentences, specific terms.

  4. 76 to 90 min

    Check: every letter used once, every answer in the right box, name on the front.

Use the timer when you do a practice paper at home, so the real timing feels normal.

Rules Test room

Test room rules

Tick each rule once you know it

  • Arrive 15 minutes early. Latecomers may not be admitted once the paper has been opened.
  • Bring your matric card and a black or blue pen. Nothing else on the desk.
  • Phones and smartwatches switched off and inside your bag at the front of the room.
  • Write your name, matric number, programme and class on the front page before you start.
  • Do not turn the page until told to. Do not leave in the final 15 minutes.
  • Any communication with another candidate ends the test for both of you.
  • Dictionaries and electronic devices are not permitted.
