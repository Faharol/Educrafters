What is technical communication?

Technical communication is the process of communicating specialised information clearly and effectively.

  • It can be written, spoken, visual, or digital.
  • It is commonly used in engineering, science, technology, business, and industry.
  • Its main goal is to help readers understand information or take appropriate action.

Examples include

Technical reports · Research articles · User manuals · Instructions · Proposals · Emails · Specifications · Safety documents

Which statement is the best description of technical communication?

In engineering

Engineering professionals communicate technical information every day

  • Explaining how a system works.
  • Reporting experimental results.
  • Describing a design.
  • Giving safety instructions.
  • Comparing engineering solutions.
  • Recommending a solution to a problem.
  • Presenting project findings.
  • Communicating with clients and colleagues.

Key idea: a technically correct idea can still fail if the reader cannot understand or use the information.

Context

Context: the situation in which communication takes place

  • Where was the document produced?
  • When was it produced?
  • Why was it produced?
  • Who requested it?
  • What problem does it address?
  • What decision may depend on it?

For example, a safety instruction has a different context from a research article. Audience, purpose, relationship, and context are important features when analysing technical discourse.

Genre

Genre: what type of document is it?

Genre means a recognisable type of communication with a particular purpose and structure.

Research article · Technical report · Laboratory report · Proposal · Manual · Specification · Memo · Email · Instruction sheet

Reading strategy: ask “What type of document am I reading?” Knowing the genre helps you predict what information will appear next.

Reading test strategies

Gapped text, matching headings, multiple choice and vocabulary in context: the Week 8 Reading Test question types.

Reading Test Gapped text

Gapped text strategy

A sentence or paragraph has been removed. Before choosing an answer:

  1. Read the whole paragraph.

  2. Read the sentence before the gap.

  3. Read the sentence after the gap.

  4. Identify the logical relationship.

  5. Look for repeated vocabulary or reference words.

  6. Check grammar.

  7. Insert the option.

  8. Read the paragraph again.

Do not choose an answer based only on one sentence.

Try it Gapped text

Which sentence fills the gap?

The system produces excessive heat. [ _____ ]

Choose the sentence that fits best.

The system overheats. [ _____ ]

Reading Test Matching headings

Matching headings strategy

For each paragraph:

  1. Read the paragraph quickly.

  2. Identify its main idea.

  3. Ignore minor examples initially.

  4. Look at the headings.

  5. Eliminate headings that are too narrow.

  6. Eliminate headings that are too broad.

  7. Choose the heading that best represents the whole paragraph.

Important: a heading should match the main idea, not simply one word in the paragraph.

Try it Matching headings

Choose the best heading

Battery performance is strongly influenced by temperature. At very low temperatures, chemical reactions inside the battery slow down, reducing its ability to deliver energy.

Which heading best represents the whole paragraph?

Reading Test Multiple choice

Multiple-choice reading strategy

  1. Read the question carefully.

  2. Identify important keywords.

  3. Locate the relevant section.

  4. Read the surrounding sentences.

  5. Eliminate clearly incorrect answers.

  6. Compare the remaining choices.

  7. Choose the answer supported by the text.

Avoid: “This answer sounds correct.”

Instead ask: “Where does the text support this answer?”

Reading Test Vocabulary in context

Technical texts contain unfamiliar words. Do not immediately use a dictionary.

First ask

  • What words appear before and after it?
  • Is there a definition?
  • Is there an example?
  • Is there a contrast?
  • Is there a cause-and-effect relationship?
  • Is the word positive or negative?
The material is brittle, meaning that it breaks easily when subjected to force.

Clarity

Clarity: the reader can understand the intended meaning

Clear technical writing uses

  • Familiar words where possible.
  • Precise technical terms when necessary.
  • Clear sentence structures.
  • Specific information.
  • Logical organisation.

NIST recommends communicating in a clear, simple, meaningful, and appropriate manner, while recognising that necessary technical terminology should not be removed when it carries important meaning. (NIST)

To make a text clearer, should you remove all technical terms?

Conciseness

Conciseness: the necessary information, without unnecessary words

Wordy: Due to the fact that the temperature was very high, the system was unable to operate properly.

Rewrite the wordy sentence in fewer words. Keep the meaning.

Check your answer
Because the temperature was high, the system could not operate properly.

“Due to the fact that” → “Because”; “was unable to” → “could not”.

Technical writing should respect the reader's time. Purdue's engineering guidance similarly recommends saying what is needed without unnecessary wording. (Purdue Engineering)

Ethics

Ethics in technical communication

It can affect people's…

Safety · Health · Money · Decisions · Environment · Professional reputation

Therefore, technical communicators should…

  • Report information honestly.
  • Avoid misleading claims.
  • Present important limitations.
  • Avoid manipulating data.
  • Acknowledge sources.
  • Correct significant errors.

Key principle: technical communication should help readers make informed decisions.
