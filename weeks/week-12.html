Assessment Speaking · 30% · Weeks 13–14

Two tasks, one score

Task 1 · 10%

Individual pitch

You get a technical problem and a target audience. You have 2 minutes to prepare, then deliver a structured 2–3 minute presentation proposing a workable solution.

Task 2 · 20%

Group negotiation

Candidates A, B, C and D hold a 12-minute discussion. Analyse a technical challenge, negotiate the trade-offs and reach a consensus on the best option.

Which task carries more of the 30%?

Task 1 · Individual presentation

A three-phase pitch

30s

Context & hook

State the technical scenario clearly. Grab attention with the immediate operational problem or its SDG relevance.

90s

Core proposal

Present your specific engineering solution. Justify it with logical, evidence-based technical or cost reasons.

30s

Call to action

Sum up the direct benefits of your proposal and invite the manager's feedback.

Task 2 · Group discussion · 12 minutes

How the 12 minutes unfold

  1. Minutes 0–3 · Initial pitches

    Each candidate briefly presents their assigned option so every technical avenue is on the table.

  2. Minutes 3–8 · Comparative analysis

    Evaluate feasibility together. Challenge options on cost, installation timeline and SDG alignment.

  3. Minutes 8–12 · Negotiation & consensus

    Reach a compromise. Agree on one practical strategy to present to the board of directors.

The trap: spending all 12 minutes arguing for your own option. The task is marked on whether the group reaches a decision together.

Marking criteria

What the examiner listens for

01

Grammatical accuracy

Accurate, natural use of complex structures, such as the passive voice and conditional clauses, with very few errors.

02

Lexical range & register

Active use of formal technical terms and precise verbs. Avoid repeating the same common phrases.

03

Fluency & pronunciation

A steady, structured pace with clear pronunciation and little hesitation.

04

Interactive effectiveness

Smooth turn-taking, active listening, negotiating views and moving the group towards a joint compromise.

Tap every card to earn the XP. Each criterion is marked out of 5 on the CEFR-aligned (ELHAM) rubric.

Weeks 13–14

Three steps to secure your speaking marks

  • Practise with timers. Record your individual presentation. Check your pace and eye contact, and cut the fillers.
  • Meet as a mock study group. Practise with your candidates, face to face or online, to build a natural rhythm.
  • Act professionally. Prepare formal clothes, bring your matric card, and arrive 15 minutes early.
