The big picture

Open, argue, share, decide

Every discussion has the same shape. Learn the five moves and you always know what to say next.

  • Open with a purpose, not a greeting.
  • Opinion needs a “because”.
  • Disagree with a reason, never just “no”.
  • Turns move on purpose.
  • Decide, or the task fails.

B

Opinions, agreement and disagreement

The exam does not reward agreement. It rewards well-supported argument, in both directions.

Part B · Opinions

State it, then support it

“In my view, the leak programme is the stronger option, because it saves the most water for a moderate cost.”
“I'd argue that cost is the deciding factor here. The board capped the budget at RM 100,000.”

Opinion first, evidence second, in one breath. An opinion without a “because” scores as a B1 turn.

Phrase bank · Agreeing and disagreeing

Five functions, and what must follow

“I completely agree.”
“That's exactly my view.”
“I'd go further and say…”

What follows: add something. Agreement alone is a wasted turn.

“I agree up to a point, but…”
“That's true for cost, although…”
“I see the logic, however…”

What follows: name the part you accept, then the part you don't.

“I'm not sure I agree, because…”
“I see it differently.”
“With respect, the figures suggest otherwise.”

What follows: always a reason. Never just “no”.

“I have to disagree.”
“I don't think that holds, given that…”
“That would be a mistake, because…”

What follows: use once, with the evidence, and then move on.

“Could you explain what you mean by…?”
“Are you saying that…?”
“Just to check, your figure of RM 85,000 includes installation?”

What follows: clarifying is a turn. It shows you are listening.

C

Turn-taking, intervention and decision

Four people, twelve minutes. The floor has to move.

Part C · Turn-taking

Signal, take, hold, pass

  1. Signal

    Show you want the floor: “May I come in here?”

  2. Take

    Come in briefly and relevantly: “Sorry to interrupt, but this is relevant to the cost point.”

  3. Hold

    Keep it without rambling: “Two quick things, then I'll hand over.”

  4. Pass

    Hand it on by name: “Farah, over to you.”

Nobody should speak for more than a minute at a time. Nobody should be silent for more than three.

Part C · Intervention

Effective intervention

Interrupting is not rude if it is done for the group. It is rude if it is done for you.

01

When to intervene

A fact is wrong. The discussion has left the task. One person has spoken for over a minute. Time is running out.

02

How to intervene

“Sorry, can I check that figure? The table says RM 85,000, not RM 58,000.” Short, specific, and back to the speaker.

03

When not to

To repeat what was just said. To agree loudly. To make a point that could wait for your turn.

04

If you are interrupted

“Let me just finish this point.” Then finish it in one sentence. Do not fight for the floor; earn it back with evidence.

Part C · Decision

Reaching a decision in five moves

  1. Summarise

    “To sum up where we are…”

  2. Weigh the options

    “On balance…”

  3. Test consensus

    “Can we all live with that?”

  4. Decide

    “Then our recommendation is…”

  5. Close

    “Thank you, everyone. We'll present this to the board as agreed.”

A discussion that ends without a decision fails the task, however fluent it was.

Marking criteria · Levels

What B2 and C1 sound like

B2

Clear, mostly accurate, can hold a turn and give a reason. Vocabulary is general; hedging is rare; interaction is polite but reactive.

C1

Precise, flexible, can reformulate on the spot. Uses technical terms naturally, hedges estimates, disagrees with evidence and moves the group towards a decision.

The difference in one turn. Here is a B2 turn: “I think leak detection is good because it is cheap.” Rewrite it at C1: add a technical term, a hedge and a figure (the budget cap is RM 100,000).

Check your answer: the C1 model
“Leak detection looks like the strongest first step: it fits the RM 100,000 cap and, judging by the table, could save up to 220 cubic metres a month.”

Notice the hedges (“looks like”, “judging by the table”, “could”) and the precise figures.

Avoid Task 2

Six ways candidates lose marks in Task 2

01

Reading a script

Prepared notes are allowed; reading them is not interaction. Look up.

02

Agreeing with everything

Four people agreeing is a monologue in four voices. Challenge something, with a reason.

03

Speaking for two minutes

The group loses turns, you lose the interaction mark, and the examiner stops listening.

04

Silence

Two minutes without a turn is a visible gap in the mark sheet. Come in with a question if nothing else.

05

Ignoring the clock

A discussion that has not reached a decision by minute 10 rarely reaches one at all. Someone must call time.

06

Forgetting the audience

You are engineers advising a board, not students chatting. Register stays formal throughout.

Practice Rules

Run the round

  1. Minutes 0 to 3

    Each candidate states their option in under 40 seconds.

  2. Minutes 3 to 8

    Compare on budget, saving and time.

  3. Minutes 8 to 12

    Decide, and assign one action.

The examiner ticks each phrase-bank function as it is used. After round one: three minutes of examiner feedback using the four criteria. Swap roles for round two. State the decision out loud before time is called.
